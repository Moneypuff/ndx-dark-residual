Part 2 — what happens to DPI after the report

The first half of this study used DPI into the print as a signal. The rest asks a different question: how does dark-pool positioning itself change once the numbers are out — one week, two weeks and a month later? Every number below is in percentage points of the DPI ratio, measured against the same event's pre-earnings DPI10, so "+1 pp" means the dark-pool short share ran one point above where it sat going into the report.

DPI spikes on the reaction day, then fades

Average DPI by session around the report, relative to that event's own pre-earnings DPI10 (the zero line). Session 0 is T, the last clean pre-news close; the news lands between 0 and +1.

The lift is a week-one phenomenon

Splitting the post-earnings month into non-overlapping slices: the elevation is concentrated in the first week and is gone by week two. Bars show the average change vs. the pre-earnings DPI10, with the two-sided p-value.

Where DPI went in, it comes back from

Events sorted into terciles by each name's DPI over an earlier window (sessions −60 to −11), which shares no observations with the change being measured. Names that went into the print with dark-pool positioning already high drift back down over the following month; those that went in low drift up.

The level carries the signal; the change doesn't

A DPI change over T+1…T+5 is only known at T+5, so the honest test is the return from that point. Against the same forward windows, the pre-earnings level keeps working and the post-earnings change adds nothing.

Read-out

Note on an earlier preliminary cut. A first pass on just 20 mega-caps over 2023–2026, with hand-typed report dates, suggested high DPI preceded a weaker next-day move. That did not survive: with authoritative EDGAR dates, AMC/BMO timing, the full 94-name universe and 8 years of history, the mega-cap next-day effect is −0.01 (i.e. noise), while the durable signal is the positive 1-month relationship shown here. The earlier result was small-sample/single-window fragility.

Method & caveats

  • Signal (no look-ahead): DPI5 / DPI10 = mean daily DPI over the 5 / 10 trading days ending the session before the report — strictly pre-announcement.
  • Outcome: next-day = adjClose(T+1)/adjClose(T)−1; 1-month = adjClose(T+21 sessions)/adjClose(T)−1, where T is the last clean pre-news close. Timing-aware: for after-hours (AMC) reports T is the report day; for before-open (BMO) reports T is the prior session, so the reaction is always the first full session on the news. Split-adjusted.
  • Dates: SEC EDGAR 8-K Item 2.02 filings, matched to each 10-Q/10-K to isolate the quarterly earnings release (dropping delivery/sales/guidance 2.02s). Acceptance timestamp → US/Eastern gives AMC vs BMO. 94 of 100 names covered; 6 foreign filers (ASML, ARM, PDD, CCEP, FER, NBIS/TRI) file 6-K, not 8-K, and are omitted.
  • Direction: a high dark-pool short-volume ratio is conventionally read as market-makers shorting to fill buy orders — i.e. accumulation. The positive DPI→forward-return link is consistent with that reading.
  • Post-earnings DPI (part 2): the same windows on the far side of the news — mean DPI over T+1…T+5 / T+10 / T+21 — minus that event's pre-earnings DPI10, in percentage points of the ratio. Session T is excluded from both sides (for an AMC reporter it is the report day itself, part pre-news tape and part after-hours reaction). Because the change contains DPI10 with a minus sign, sorting events on DPI10 exaggerates mean reversion; the tercile sorts use an earlier, non-overlapping window (sessions −60…−11) instead.
  • Change as a signal: a change over T+1…T+5 is only observable at T+5, so it is tested against the return from T+5 to T+21 (and T+10→T+21, T+21→T+42) — never against a window it overlaps.
  • Caveats: effect sizes are small (r≈0.01 next-day, ≈0.06 at a month) — a real but modest tilt, not a stand-alone trade. Single index, mostly-bull sample. DPI is a noisy daily series.
